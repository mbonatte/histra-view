The arc-length recommendation also contains a scale inconsistency: its example

case. No routine assertion is relaxed.

## Performance evidence

The small-copy finding is actionable without removing buffer protection.
Eight paired five-commit model-live runs, with an explicit ModifiedNewtonRaphson
method, produce identical committed arrays and scalar fields. Median measured
solve time is 0.10050 s with always-reuse versus 0.08381 s with size selection.
This is one small configuration, not a universal speedup. Raw samples and the
microbenchmark are in [compact evidence](../../release-evidence/performance/2026-10-15-small-snapshot-adaptive.json).

The review's very large initialization/Vert speed ratios mix first-run JIT
effects with implementation performance, as its own explanation acknowledges.
They cannot establish a general warm-solver speedup without separate cold and
warm samples, identical inputs, cache state, outputs and stopping checks. Its
different large-snapshot ratio does not contradict the separately recorded
Windows profile; the platforms, sample sizes and conditions differ.

The [full fast-library experiments](../benchmarks/faster-linear-library-screen-2026-10-15.md)
also show why near-identical early response is insufficient. Fast UMFPACK stops
the horizontal model seven commits early; SuperLU commits a step that the
native reference does not. Both are rejected for complete-history qualification.
